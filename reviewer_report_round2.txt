## Summary

This B.Sc. engineering thesis presents a Hybrid Adaptive Transformer Differential Protection (HATDP) framework that integrates multi-resolution Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) feature extraction and second-harmonic restraint ratios with a Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) sequence classifier. Specifically, the authors extract nine log-compressed two-level Daubechies-4 (`db4`) wavelet energy features and three phase-wise second-harmonic restraint ratios over a 32-sample sliding window to drive a two-layer unidirectional attention-LSTM network embedded within a dual-path supervisory relay architecture (combining an instantaneous high-set element, an 87T-gated handshake, and a 20-sample dwell-filtered autonomous AI path). Evaluated across 3,218 simulated electromagnetic-transient (EMT) traces on a 300 MVA, 230/11 kV, Yd1 transformer model and an out-of-distribution (OOD) stress suite, the framework reconciles high security against magnetizing inrush and current transformer (CT) saturation with improved dependability on sub-pickup high-impedance internal faults, while verifying closed-loop software co-simulation parity in MATLAB/Simulink via ONNX.



## Strengths

* **Pragmatic Dual-Path Supervisory Relay Architecture:** Rather than deploying the neural network as an unanchored black-box trip element or a purely serial veto that sacrifices sensitivity on weak faults, the proposed dual-path Boolean handshake pairs an ANSI/IEEE 87T percentage-restraint confirmation path with a persistence-filtered autonomous AI path ($\theta_{\text{solo}} \ge 0.998$ over a 12.5 ms dwell timer), recovering 71.2% of high-impedance internal faults in the 87T-invisible zone ($R_f = 1.2\text{--}7.0\,\Omega$) while preserving 99.66% security.

* **Physically Grounded Feature Fusion and Compact Sequence Modeling:** Embedding domain-specific second-harmonic restraint ratios alongside log-compressed two-level `db4` sub-band energies into a compact 58,241-parameter attention-LSTM (~1.71 MFLOPs/inference) provides physically interpretable transient trajectory tracking across successive windows within sub-cycle computational budgets.

* **Rigorous Evaluation Protocol and Closed-Loop Co-Simulation Parity:** The study enforces leak-free trace-level cross-validation, segregates physically undetectable sub-noise-floor faults ($<0.05\text{ pu}$) to avoid metric inflation, evaluates out-of-distribution stress conditions, and verifies bit-exact closed-loop execution parity between Python/PyTorch and MATLAB/Simulink via ONNX.



## Weaknesses

* **Internal Contradictions in Reported Metrics and Architectural Specifications:** Please consider reconciling the discrepancies between the main text/Table 6.1 (reporting 99.01% dependability, 99.66% security, and 6 false trips from the 3-repetition mean) and Table 6.7 Panel B (reporting 98.56% dependability, 99.41% security, and 11 false trips from Repetition 1), correcting the narrative in Section 6.10 where cited false-trip reductions do not match Table 6.7, and updating Figures 5.1–5.7 so they reflect the deployed 12-feature, 64/64-unit, 58,241-parameter binary network rather than an earlier 6-feature, 128/64-unit, 125,476-parameter 4-class prototype.

* **Artificial Current Limiting of External Through-Faults and 87T Restraint Bias:** Inserting a $0.05\,\Omega$ series limiting resistor in the external LV network (Section 4.4) to cap external through-fault currents at $\approx 1.5\text{ pu}$ rms while inducing CT saturation via extreme burden sweeps ($4\times\text{--}16\times$ nominal) dampens the natural primary $L/R$ DC time constant and restricts the conventional 87T relay to operating below its $2.0\text{ A}$ knee point in the sensitive Slope 1 region ($15\%$), denying it the high-restraint Slope 2 region ($40\%$) intended by industry standards and biasing the baseline security evaluation (Reference Paper: IEEE Guide for Protecting Power Transformers (IEEE Std C37.91-2021), IEEE Power and Energy Society, 2021).

* **Incremental Algorithmic Novelty of the Feature-LSTM Pipeline:** Because combining Fourier/harmonic indices with DWT features for machine learning classifiers and feeding multi-resolution wavelet features with relay quantities into LSTM or 1D-CNN/LSTM networks under CT saturation are well-established in recent literature, the authors might consider framing the core contribution more explicitly around the dual-path supervisory handshake and closed-loop verification rather than the feature-LSTM pipeline alone (Reference Paper: A New Hybrid Machine Learning Method for Detecting Faults in Power Transformers, M. Abdusalam, K. F. Ali, and W. M. El-Hawary, 2022; Reference Paper: A Novel Approach for Diagnosing Transformer Internal Defects and Inrush Current Based on 1DCNN and LSTM Deep Learning, W. A. Atiyah, S. Karimi, and M. Moradi, 2023; Reference Paper: Advanced Fault Detection in Power Transformers Using Improved Wavelet Analysis and LSTM Networks Considering Current Transformer Saturation and Uncertainties, Q. Alhamd, M. Saniei, S. G. Seifossadat, and E. Mashhour, 2024).

* **Omission of Modern Deterministic Wavelet Baselines:** In Table 6.7, static classifiers (Wavelet-ANN and Wavelet-RF) already capture most of the accuracy gain over the legacy 1958 harmonic-restraint relay, so the authors might consider adding a modern deterministic wavelet-domain baseline—such as the Boundary Discrete Wavelet Transform (BDWT) or Clarke-wavelet schemes already cited in Section 2.3—to isolate the benefit of the LSTM sequence model from the wavelet representation itself (Reference Paper: A new approach to transformer differential protection, R. L. Sharp and W. E. Glassburn, 1958; Reference Paper: Power transformer differential protection using the boundary discrete wavelet transform, R. P. Medeiros, F. B. Costa, and K. M. Silva, 2016; Reference Paper: A Clarke-Wavelet-Based Time-Domain Power Transformer Differential Protection, R. P. Medeiros, F. B. Costa, K. M. Silva, J. J. Chavez, J. R. Lima Junior, and M. Popov, 2022).

* **Single-Plant Software Simulation Scope vs. Hardware-in-the-Loop Validation:** While closed-loop ONNX co-simulation in Simulink is a commendable software-in-the-loop verification step using standard vendor tools, all evaluations are confined to virtual simulation time on a single synthetic 300 MVA plant; validating across multi-topology benchmarks and discussing physical Hardware-in-the-Loop (HIL) testing on RTDS/OPAL-RT platforms would strengthen claims of industrial real-time readiness (Reference Paper: Interoperability Between Deep Learning Toolbox, TensorFlow, PyTorch, and ONNX, The MathWorks, Inc., 2024; Reference Paper: Integration of Accelerated Deep Neural Network into Power Transformer Differential Protection, S. Afrasiabi, M. Afrasiabi, B. Parang, and M. Mohammadi, 2020; Reference Paper: A Clarke-Wavelet-Based Time-Domain Power Transformer Differential Protection, R. P. Medeiros, F. B. Costa, K. M. Silva, J. J. Chavez, J. R. Lima Junior, and M. Popov, 2022).

* **Bibliographic and Citation Integrity Discrepancies:** Several in-text citations in Chapter 2 do not match the methods of their referenced bibliography entries (e.g., citing microgrid EWT and energy-tracking papers for core flux equations, or WPT/ANN papers for DFT limitations), multiple bibliography entries contain inaccurate metadata (venue names, volume/page numbers, or author lists), and nine listed references are never cited in the text; a thorough audit of all 40 references and the List of Publications is recommended.

1. **Reference Base, Dimensional Consistency, and Impedance Quadrature in the Maximum Detectable Fault Resistance Derivation (Section 3.2.3, Equations 3.3–3.4, Page 10 [PDF Page 29]):**

   In Section 3.2.3, the symmetrical-component single-line-to-ground (SLG) fault current is written as

   $$I_f = \frac{3V_f}{|Z_1 + Z_2 + Z_0 + 3R_f|} \quad (3.3)$$

   and the maximum fault resistance detectable by conventional percentage differential protection is approximated by setting $I_d = I_{\text{pickup}}$:

   $$R_{f,\max} \approx \frac{V_f}{I_{\text{pickup}}} - \frac{|Z_1 + Z_2 + Z_0|}{3}, \quad (3.4)$$

   followed by the conclusion that $R_f \ge 1.2\,\Omega$ in the $300\text{ MVA}$ plant leaves the fault undetectable by standard 87T elements ($I_d < I_{\text{pickup}}$). There are three potential mathematical and physical concerns with this derivation as written:

   - **Primary vs. Secondary / Per-Unit Scaling and Winding Reference Frame:** In the List of Symbols (Page ix [PDF Page 10]) and Figure 3.1 (Page 10 [PDF Page 29]), $I_{\text{pickup}}$ is specified in CT secondary amperes ($I_{\text{pickup}} = 0.3\text{ A}$, or $0.30\text{ pu}$ of the $0.9302\text{ A}$ rated secondary base in Figure 6.2 and Table 4.2), whereas $V_f$, $Z_1, Z_2, Z_0$, and $R_f$ in Equations (3.3)–(3.4) are primary phase-to-ground voltage and ohmic impedance quantities in a dual-voltage ($230\text{ kV} / 11\text{ kV}$) transformer. Specifically, the stated limit $R_{f,\max} \approx 1.2\,\Omega$ only holds when evaluated on the $11\text{ kV}$ low-voltage (LV) primary base ($V_{f,\text{LV}} = 11\text{ kV}/\sqrt{3} \approx 6{,}351\text{ V}$, $Z_{\text{base,LV}} = 0.4033\,\Omega$, $I_{\text{FLA,LV}} = 15{,}746\text{ A}$, where a $0.30\text{ pu}$ pickup corresponds to $I_{\text{pickup,LV}} \approx 4{,}724\text{ A}$ primary and $V_{f,\text{LV}} / I_{\text{pickup,LV}} \approx 1.34\,\Omega$; on the $230\text{ kV}$ HV side, $Z_{\text{base,HV}} = 176.33\,\Omega$, scaling the ohmic threshold by $(230/11)^2 \approx 437\times$). Furthermore, passing from the numerator $3V_f$ in Equation (3.3) to $V_f / I_{\text{pickup}}$ in Equation (3.4) implicitly equates $I_{\text{pickup}}$ to $I_f / 3$ (the sequence current $I_{a1} = I_{a2} = I_{a0}$) rather than the total fault current $I_f$, without accounting for the CT turns ratio $N_{CT}$ or the $1/\sqrt{3}$ Clarke delta-to-wye compensation matrix applied to the LV line currents (Equation 4.1, Page 16 [PDF Page 35]).

   - **Delta-Connected LV Winding Zero-Sequence Topology:** Because the $11\text{ kV}$ LV winding of the $\text{Yd1}$ transformer is delta-connected, the zero-sequence network is isolated ($Z_0 \to \infty$) for ungrounded internal LV single-line-to-ground faults, whereas phase-to-phase faults on the delta winding (such as those plotted in Figure 6.2) follow $I_f = \sqrt{3}V_f / |Z_1 + Z_2 + R_f|$.

   - **Collinear Impedance Approximation:** Passing from Equation (3.3) to Equation (3.4) replaces $|Z_1 + Z_2 + Z_0 + 3R_f|$ with the scalar sum $|Z_1 + Z_2 + Z_0| + 3R_f$. Because the system and transformer sequence impedances $Z_1, Z_2, Z_0$ are predominantly inductive ($jX_{\Sigma}$) while $3R_f$ is purely resistive, they add in quadrature: $\sqrt{(R_{\Sigma} + 3R_f)^2 + X_{\Sigma}^2}$.

   - **Suggested Improvement:** Explicitly state the per-unit / primary-referred conversion and the winding side ($11\text{ kV}$ LV base) used to obtain $R_{f,\max} \approx 1.2\,\Omega$, clarify the mapping between primary fault current $I_f$ and compensated relay differential current $I_d$, and note the high-$R_f$ collinear approximation.

3. **Turn-Fraction Fault Impedance Notation and Autotransformer Voltage Scaling in Internal Winding Faults (Section 3.5, Equation 3.8, Page 12 [PDF Page 31]):**

   - **Potential Concern:** Section 3.5 states that when an internal fault occurs at turn fraction $p \in (0, 1]$ of the winding, the fault impedance scales quadratically with the shorted turns as

     $$Z_{\text{fault}}(p) = p^2 Z_t + R_f, \quad (3.8)$$

     and immediately follows with: *"Using symmetrical components, an SLG fault yields $I_{a1} = I_{a2} = I_{a0} = V_f / (Z_1 + Z_2 + Z_0 + 3Z_f)$."* This formulation presents two ambiguities:

     1. *Undefined and Inconsistent Impedance Symbols:* The transformer winding impedance symbol $Z_t$ in Equation (3.8) and the fault impedance symbol $Z_f$ in the inline equation are not defined in the text or the List of Symbols, leaving it ambiguous whether $Z_f \equiv R_f$ (matching Equation 3.3, where $Z_1, Z_2, Z_0$ already contain the winding impedance) or $Z_f \equiv Z_{\text{fault}}(p)$ (which would double-count the transformer impedance if $Z_1, Z_2, Z_0$ also include it).

     2. *Turn-Fraction Driving Voltage and Lumped-Parameter Scope:* While the self-impedance of a shorted winding section scales approximately as $p^2 Z_t$, an internal turn-to-ground fault at fraction $p$ of a grounded winding acts as an autotransformer where the pre-fault driving voltage at the fault point also scales with $p$ ($V_f(p) = p V_{\text{terminal}}$) and the resulting fault current referred to the terminal CTs scales by the turns ratio. 4. **Exponential Decay Applied to Remanent Core Flux in Equation (3.5) and Continuity of the Nonlinear CT Excitation Curve in Equation (3.6) (Sections 3.3.1 and 3.4, Pages 10–11 [PDF Pages 29–30]):**

   - **Core Flux Decay and Peak Flux Approximation (Equation 3.5):** Under sinusoidal excitation $v(t) = V_m \sin(\omega t + \alpha)$, Equation (3.5) expresses the instantaneous core flux as

     $$\phi(t) = -\Phi_m \cos(\omega t + \alpha) + \Phi_m \cos(\alpha)e^{-t/\tau} + \phi_r e^{-t/\tau}. \quad (3.5)$$

     In ideal Faraday integration $\phi(t) = \phi_r + \frac{1}{N}\int_0^t v(u)\,du$, the remanent flux $\phi_r$ enters as a static initial magnetic condition prior to core saturation, while the circuit $L/R$ damping factor $e^{-t/\tau}$ governs the decay of the total DC flux offset $(\Phi_m \cos\alpha + \phi_r)e^{-t/\tau}$ once the core saturates and draws magnetizing current through the primary winding resistance. Furthermore, on Page 11 [PDF Page 30], the worst-case peak flux ($\alpha = 0, \phi_r = +\Phi_m$) is written as $\phi_{\text{peak}} = 2\Phi_m + \phi_r = 3\Phi_m$. Obtaining this exact expression from Equation (3.5) requires evaluating $\phi(t)$ one half-cycle after energization ($\omega t = \pi$, or $t = 10\text{ ms}$ at $50\text{ Hz}$) under the lightly damped approximation $e^{-\pi/(\omega\tau)} \approx 1$ ($\tau \gg T/2$). Stating this half-cycle lightly damped condition explicitly is recommended.

   - **Origin Discontinuity in the CT Excitation Model (Equation 3.6):** Equation (3.6) models the nonlinear CT magnetizing current as a function of flux linkage $\lambda = N_2 \phi$:

     $$i_e(\lambda) = A e^{B|\lambda|}\operatorname{sign}(\lambda) + C\lambda. \quad (3.6)$$

     Because $e^{B|\lambda|} \to 1$ as $\lambda \to 0$, this formula has a step discontinuity of $2A$ at the origin ($\lim_{\lambda \to 0^{\pm}} i_e(\lambda) = \pm A$ for $A \neq 0$). If an anhysteretic single-valued saturation curve passing continuously through the origin ($i_e(0) = 0$) is intended, the standard exponential form is $i_e(\lambda) = A(e^{B|\lambda|} - 1)\operatorname{sign}(\lambda) + C\lambda$; conversely, if $A$ is intended to represent a coercive hysteresis offset, this should be stated explicitly along with the units of the fitting parameters $A, B, C$.

   - **MACs vs. FLOPs per Inference in Table 5.2:** In Table 5.2, the column header is labeled `FLOPs/step`, while the caption discusses the per-inference operation budget across the 32-step window. Specifically, the reported values for `LSTM layer 1 (12 -> 64)` ($\approx 0.63\text{ M}$) and `LSTM layer 2 (64 -> 64)` ($\approx 1.06\text{ M}$) equal $32 \times 19,712 = 630,784$ and $32 \times 33,024 = 1,056,768$, which correspond to counting $1\text{ MAC} = 1\text{ FLOP}$ across all 32 sequence steps of a full inference window (whereas counting $2\text{ FLOPs/MAC}$ yields $\approx 1.26\text{ M}$ and $\approx 2.11\text{ M}$ FLOPs). Additionally, for the `Dense Classifier (64 -> 64 -> 1)` applied once after global attention pooling, `~17.5 k` does not match $1\times$ or $2\times$ the `5,312` parameters. Clarifying the MAC/FLOP counting convention and renaming the column header from `FLOPs/step` to `FLOPs/inference` (or `MACs/inference`) would improve table precision.



3. **Wavelet Coefficient Count at Decomposition Level 2 Under Symmetric Boundary Extension (Section 5.4, Table 5.1, Page 41):**

   In Section 5.4 (p. 22, PDF p. 41), the text states that a two-level Daubechies-4 (`db4`, filter length $L = 8$) decomposition is applied to a $W = 32$-sample window using symmetric reflection boundary extension (`mode='symmetric'`), and Table 5.1 reports `# Coeff. = 19` at Level 1 ($d_1$) and `# Coeff. = 11` at Level 2 ($a_2$ and $d_2$). Under standard dyadic convolution and downsampling with symmetric boundary extension (e.g., PyWavelets `pywt.wavedec(x, 'db4', mode='symmetric', level=2)`), each decomposition level on an input of length $N_{j-1}$ with filter length $L = 8$ produces $N_j = \lfloor (N_{j-1} + L - 1)/2 \rfloor$ coefficients:

   - Level 1 ($N_0 = 32, L = 8$): $N_1 = \lfloor (32 + 8 - 1)/2 \rfloor = 19$ coefficients (matching `19` for $d_1$ in Table 5.1).

   - Level 2 ($N_1 = 19, L = 8$): $N_2 = \lfloor (19 + 8 - 1)/2 \rfloor = 13$ coefficients for $d_2$ and $a_2$, whereas Table 5.1 lists `11` coefficients for both $a_2$ and $d_2$ (a count of `11` corresponds to $\lfloor (16 + 8 - 1)/2 \rfloor = 11$ from a 16-sample input window, or trimming two boundary coefficients prior to Level 2).

   Because the sub-band energy feature $E = \ln(1 + \sum |c[k]|^2)$ is an unnormalized sum over $k$ and therefore depends directly on the number of coefficients summed, clarifying whether `# Coeff.` at Level 2 in Table 5.1 should be `13` or whether boundary coefficients are trimmed at Level 2 is important for exact feature replicability.



4. **Ambiguity in Sliding-Window Buffer Accumulation vs. Single-Window Horizon and Feature Normalization (Sections 5.1–5.4, Pages 38–41):**

   Two methodological details regarding how the `[1, 32, 12]` input tensor is constructed and scaled could be stated more explicitly to prevent ambiguity during reimplementation:

   - **Cascaded Window and Sequence Buffer Horizon:** Sections 5.1 and 5.2 state that a 32-sample full-cycle sliding window ($W = 32$, $20\text{ ms}$ at $1.6\text{ kHz}$) forms an input tensor of dimension `[1, 32, 12]`. Meanwhile, Sections 5.2.2 (Stages 2–4), 5.3, and 5.4 explain that at each sample step $t$ ($\Delta t = 0.625\text{ ms}$), a 32-sample window is used to compute one 12-dimensional feature vector $f(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{12}$, which is then *"accumulated over the full-cycle buffer into $X(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{32 \times 12}$."* Because stacking 32 consecutive window-level feature vectors $[f(t-31), \dots, f(t)]^\top$—each computed over a 32-sample raw current window—spans a total historical lookback of $32 + 32 - 1 = 63$ raw samples ($39.375\text{ ms}$) once fully populated, explicitly stating this total lookback span and specifying how the sequence buffer is initialized during the first 31 feature steps (e.g., edge-replication or zero-padding of $f(t)$) would remove any ambiguity.

   - **Input Current Units and Feature Standardization:** Figure 5.1 (Step 1b) mentions `Z-score normalization`, whereas Section 5.4 defines the log-compressed wavelet energies $E = \ln(1 + \sum |c[k]|^2)$ and capped second-harmonic ratios $k_{2,\varphi} \in [0, 2.0]$ without mentioning subsequent z-score standardization. Furthermore, because $\ln(1 + \sum |c[k]|^2)$ is non-linear and scale-dependent due to the additive constant $1$, explicitly stating whether the raw differential current fed into the DWT filter bank is in per-unit ($\text{pu}$, relative to $I_{\text{nom,sec}} = 0.9302\text{ A}$) or secondary Amperes, and whether z-score standardization using training-fold statistics is applied to $f(t)$ prior to `LSTM-1`, would ensure exact numerical replicability.
