## Title (draft)

A model-agnostic dual-path supervisory logic for transformer differential protection, evaluated with a leak-free benchmark of six reproduced methods

## Abstract (draft)

Published learning-based transformer differential relays are rarely compared under the same data, folds and decision logic. We evaluate on simulated 300 MVA and 40 MVA plants with trace-grouped cross-validation (3 seeds x 5 folds, 3,302 traces) and re-implement six published methods, trained on our data at each paper's own sampling rate. Natively, none of the six reaches 93 % dependability and 98.8 % security together: the two most dependable (Key et al., Alhamd et al.) reach 97.0 % and 96.5 % at only 75.3 % and 82.9 % security, and the most secure (Abdusalam et al.) reaches 76.4 % dependability. We then study a dual-path supervisory logic (87T vote, confirmed classifier output, AI-alone path, high-set) with interchangeable classifiers. In hybrid mode, six instances (1D-CNN, GRU, ANN, Key's network, RF, LSTM; equal 12-configuration search, equal training budget) give 93.5-95.7 % dependability and 98.8-99.9 % security, and the deployed LSTM instance used in our Simulink closed loop gives 93.0 % and 99.0 %. All six are significantly more dependable than a validation-tuned harmonic-restrained 87T (91.2 % / 99.1 %) by 2.3-4.6 percentage points (Holm-corrected, post-hoc), with security within -0.3 to +0.8 points. Transfer to an unseen plant depends on the classifier: zero-shot security on the 40 MVA plant is 99.9 % with Key's network but 88.4 % with the deployed LSTM, whose AI-alone path caused 87 of 89 false trips. High-impedance faults remain the limit: about 65 % dependability for the hybrid and 62 % for the tuned conventional relay. Only a kernel SVM is clearly worse (87.0 % / 97.7 %). Among the other classifiers the choice is not decisive; the supervisory logic and the evaluation protocol are the contribution.
