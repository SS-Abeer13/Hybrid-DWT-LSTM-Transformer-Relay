Paper: E. Hajipour, M. Vakilian, M. Written 2026-11-01 before any code or result of this stage.
Module (to be written): `lstm_models/baselines/hajipour2015_ct_comp.py`. It is used (a) as the first stage of the Afrasiabi et al. CNN (their Sec. II-A calls it "an LS-based wave shape-independent method comprised of two filters") and (b) tested on its own against the true primary current.

## 1. What the method is
Two causal, sample-by-sample filters: **DSC** (deformed signal compensation) reconstructs the primary current of a CT that saturates under a fault current (chopped waveform); **DOCC** (DC offset current compensation) removes the average magnetising current of a CT that drifts into saturation under a decaying DC component (inrush, low-magnitude faults). (Sec. II, Fig. 1)

## 3. DOCC (Sec. IV)
Detection of DC-offset saturation: an LES filter gives the AC magnitude I_mag, an averaging filter the DC offset I_DC, both on a window of 8 samples; DC-offset saturation when |I_mag[n]| < ε1 and |I_DC[n]| > ε2 (ε1, ε2 "pre-defined constants"). I_shift[n] = 0 if |I_mag[n]| > ε1, I_DC[n] otherwise (Eq. 12); I_bias[n] = -sum_{i=0}^{N-1} |I_shift[n-i]| / sum_{i=0}^{N-1} sgn(I_shift[n-i]) over one cycle N (Eq. 13), added to the output of the DSC block at every sample.

## 4. UNSPECIFIED items and how they are filled
| Item | Fill |
|---|---|
| Sampling rate | 4,000 Hz (80 samples per cycle at 50 Hz); anti-aliased resampling from 20 kHz replaces the analogue Butterworth filter. The compensated signal is resampled to the CNN's 5.6 kHz afterwards. |
| CT parameters (L_inf, λ_0, λ_sat, R_CT, R_B, L_B) | from the CT blocks of the plant (the routine-test values the paper asks for): saturation points of the saturable-transformer curve, with the flux base V2 sqrt2 / ω and the current base I2n sqrt2 (checked against our data: fitted slope 110.8 A/Wb and intercept 0.240 Wb on saturated plant-A traces versus 113.1 A/Wb and 0.239 Wb from the curve). L_inf and λ_0 from the last two points of the curve, the saturation point from the second-to-last point. Plant A: R_CT 0.2 ohm, nominal burden 1 ohm, V2 5 V, I2n 5 A; plant B: R_CT 0.125 ohm, nominal burden 2.5 ohm, V2 2.5 V, I2n 1 A. L_B = 0. |
| Burden used by the algorithm | the **nominal** burden. The CT-stress traces of our data have 2-6 (plant A) or 3.5-8 (plant B) times the nominal burden, which the relay would not know; the resulting mismatch is a property of the test and is reported (an oracle variant that uses the true burden is run only in the sanity check, to separate the effect). |
| Δr, Δb | Δr = 10 % (resistive burden), Δb = I2n / 4 (1.25 A on plant A, 0.25 A on plant B). |
| LES model | three parameters (constant DC, cosine and sine of the fundamental) on the last 12 unsaturated samples; the paper's statement that 12 samples "consider up to the sixth harmonic" is not used further. |
| ε1, ε2, window for DOCC | ε1 = ε2 = 0.05 I2n; windows as stated (8 samples; one cycle for I_bias). DOCC acts on DC-offset saturation of the inrush kind, which does not occur in our traces (our CTs are linear at inrush: CT ratio error of inrush traces is a few per cent), so it is **off by default** and its effect is only reported in the sanity check. |
| Initial conditions | the flux recursion starts at every saturation inception from the LS estimate; no memory between saturation episodes. |
| Three phases and two sides | the filter runs independently on each of the six CT secondary currents. |

## 5. Sanity check
Reproduce the paper's transient-error measure (Eq. 14): |K i_s^e(t) - i_p(t)| / I_psc x 100 % with the ideal secondary current K i_p = true current / nominal ratio and I_psc the AC magnitude of the primary current, on the saturated CT-stress external-fault traces of plant A. Report the median and maximum over traces with nominal burden assumed, with the true burden (oracle), and without compensation. Save the figure of one trace as `documents/baselines/hajipour2015_sanity.png` (secondary, reconstructed, true).

## 6. Result of the sanity check (2026-11-01, `tools/hajipour_sanity.py`, output `hajipour2015_sanity.txt/.png`; 40 CT-stress external-fault traces of plant A, first blocks of the main set)
Transient error (Eq. 14, worst phase), median / 90th percentile / maximum: no compensation 7.88 / 71.72 / 86.34 %; compensation with the nominal burden 7.88 / 66.99 / 86.34 %; with the true burden 7.88 / 66.99 / 86.34 % (the paper reports below 1.4-3.2 % in its worst cases). The filter as specified therefore **does not reproduce the paper's accuracy on our CT-stress traces**: the third-difference detector (Eq. 9-11) only fires late, mostly at kinks where saturation ends, because our CT (soft-knee curve, burdens of 2-6 times nominal, 4 kHz sampling, noise) saturates gradually; the 12-sample LES window then already contains saturated samples, and the LS flux estimate at t_E is below the saturation-flux in most episodes. Added guard (not in the paper, found necessary): an episode whose estimated |lambda| is not above lambda_sat is treated as a false alarm and nothing is compensated; without it the flux recursion drifted to magnetising currents of 60 A (observed on the unguarded run: median error unchanged, p90 124 %, max 237 % with the true burden). The reproduction is reported as it is; no parameters were tuned to improve it (rule 2.8). Both variants of the CNN (with / without this stage) go into the results.
